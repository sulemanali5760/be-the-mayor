# Be the Mayor: real-world research

The research lane for game design. It covers how an ordinary person really moves from labourer to mayor to president, with numbers.

- **Countries compared:** Germany (DE), USA (US) and Pakistan (PK), with India (IN) where the data is better.
- **Date:** research done September 2026. Figures are the latest ones found online. Every source is linked inline.
- **Marking:** anything labelled **(est.)** is a rough estimate. It comes from knowledge or from a non-official source, not from a statistical office. Treat these as tuning values, not facts.
- **Currency** (rough, est.): 1 USD ≈ 0.86-0.92 EUR ≈ 280 PKR.

## 1. Ordinary life

### 1.1 Entry-level jobs: pay and hours

**Sources**
- US: [BLS OEWS May 2023 national table](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes_nat.htm). Newer BLS years are published only as zip files.
- DE minimum wage: €13.90 since 1 Jan 2026, €14.60 from 2027 ([Bundesregierung](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/mindestlohn-steigt-2391010)).
- DE cleaning-industry minimum: €15.00 (group 1) to €18.40 (group 6) in 2026 ([reinigungsmarketing.com summary](https://www.reinigungsmarketing.com/ratgeber/tariflohn-gebaeudereinigung-2026)).
- PK minimum wage: Rs 40,000/month for unskilled work in Punjab and Sindh for FY 2025-26 ([commoner-law.com guide](https://commoner-law.com/pakistan/workers-rights/minimum-wage)). Karachi day rates come from a contractor blog ([nfsons.com](https://nfsons.com/blog/construction-labour-rate-karachi)), so they are estimates.
- PK household income: [HIES 2024-25 via Business Recorder](https://www.brecorder.com/news/40403008).

**Hours**
- US full-time workers work about 8.1 hours on the days they work.
- In DE a normal contract is about 38-40 hours a week.
- In PK many informal jobs run 10-12 hours a day, six days a week (est.). Minimum wages are weakly enforced in the informal sector, which is most urban work there (est.).

**Game note:** in PK, a day labourer is paid only on days there is work. A "no work today" roll for day labour is realistic.

### 1.2 Living costs as a share of spending or income

| Item | US (share of all spending, 2024) | DE | PK (share of spending, 2024-25) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Housing | 33.4% (about $2,189/month) | Renters pay 27.8% of net income on gross cold rent (2022) | Roughly 20-30% for urban renters (est.) |
| Food | 12.9% (about $847/month) | About 12-15% of spending (est.) | 36.7%, the biggest item |
| Transport | 17.0% (about $1,110/month) | About 12-14% (est.) | About 5-8% (est.) |

**Sources**
- US: [BLS, "Housing and transportation accounted for 50 percent of household spending in 2024"](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2026/housing-and-transportation-accounted-for-50-percent-of-household-spending-in-2024.htm).
- DE: [Destatis press release 2023: Mietbelastungsquote 27.8%](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2023/03/PD23_129_12_63.html).
- PK: [HIES 2024-25 final report (PBS)](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/household-integrated-economic-survey-hies-2024-25/). The food share of 36.72% is quoted in the report summaries.

**Game rule of thumb:** the poorer the player, the bigger the share of income that goes on food.
- A PK labourer's wage is mostly eaten by food and rent.
- A US labourer's wage is mostly eaten by rent and a car.
- A DE labourer is squeezed mainly by rent in cities.

### 1.3 How days are actually spent (time-use surveys)

**US: [American Time Use Survey](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.htm)** (latest release; average over all people aged 15+, all days)
- Sleep: about 9.0 h. This includes time in bed.
- Leisure and sport: 5.2 h, of which TV is 2.6 h.
- Work: 3.3 h averaged over everyone. People who worked that day averaged about 8 h.
- Housework: 0.6 h.
- Full-time workers work about 8.5 h on a weekday and 5.5 h on a weekend day they work.
- Adults with a child under 6 spend about 2.3 h a day on childcare.

**DE: [Destatis Zeitverwendungserhebung 2022](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Themen/Gesellschaft-Umwelt/Einkommen-Konsum-Lebensbedingungen/Zeitverwendung/Ergebnisse/_inhalt.html)**
- Roughly half of the day goes on basic needs: sleep, eating, hygiene.
- Adults do about **45 h a week of total work**, paid plus unpaid.
- Unpaid work: women about 30 h a week, men about 21 h ([Destatis 2024](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2024/02/PD24_073_63991.html)).
- Parents work about 10.5 h a week more in total than people without children.

**IN: [Time Use Survey 2024 (MoSPI/PIB)](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2106113&reg=3&lang=2)**
- In a given 24 hours, 75% of men and 25% of women aged 15-59 did paid or employment-related work.
- Women who did unpaid domestic work spent about 305 minutes a day on it.
- Self-care and maintenance take about 708 minutes a day.
- Leisure, culture and media take 11% of the day.

**Game translation, a 24 h budget for a working adult (est.)**
- Sleep 8 h, work plus commute 9-10 h, chores and care 2-3 h, eating and hygiene 2 h.
- That leaves **about 1-3 free hours on a workday** and 5-7 on a day off.
- "Self-improvement" and "helping people" should compete for those free hours.
- Children and care duties shrink them. This matches the survey data.

### 1.4 What realistically holds people back

- **Debt.**
  - DE: 5.67 million adults (8.13%) were over-indebted in 2025, the first rise since 2018 ([Creditreform SchuldnerAtlas 2025](https://www.creditreform.de/aktuelles-wissen/pressemeldungen-fachbeitraege/news-details/show/schuldneratlas-deutschland-2025)).
  - US: about 15 million people had about $49 bn of medical bills on their credit reports. A 2025 rule to remove them was later blocked ([CFPB archive](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-finds-15-million-americans-have-medical-bills-on-their-credit-reports/)).
- **Housing.** Rent is the largest fixed cost in DE and the US (see 1.2). Moving to a cheaper area usually means a longer commute.
- **Health and fatigue.**
  - Manual jobs wear the body down.
  - Mothers in PK and IN do 5+ hours of unpaid work a day.
  - In DE, 41% of mayors say the job has hurt their health (see section 5).
  - (est.) Injury risk is highest in construction and delivery.
- **Informality (PK and IN).** Most urban jobs have no contract, no sick pay and no pension (est.). One illness or one lost day can wipe out a week's savings.
- **Qualifications.** Without a recognised qualification, pay stays flat for life. In DE, unskilled full-time workers earn a median of about €1.0-2.6 million over a working life, depending on the job ([IAB Kurzbericht 18/2022](https://doku.iab.de/kurzber/2022/kb2022-18.pdf)). A trade or degree lifts the ceiling (see 2.2).

**Game mechanics these suggest:** an energy meter drained by work, a debt meter with interest, a rent due every week, and random "illness" and "no work today" events. Poorer settings should have higher odds of these events.

## 2. Upgrading yourself

### 2.1 Real paths from labourer upward

| Path | DE | US | PK |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Trade apprenticeship** (electrician, mason, plumber) | *Ausbildung*, 3-3.5 years, dual system of company plus school. Paid while learning: average €1,209/month in 2025 ([BIBB](https://www.bibb.de/de/12209.php)) | Registered apprenticeship, 4-5 years (about 8,000 h on the job plus classroom). Starts at about 40-50% of a qualified worker's rate (est.) | Mostly informal: an *ustad-shagird* (master and apprentice) arrangement for 1-3 years at low pay (est.). Formal TEVTA/NAVTTC courses last 3-12 months, are often free and some pay a stipend (est.) |
| **Qualified tradesperson** | *Geselle*. Electrician median €3,935/month gross ([Entgeltatlas, Elektroniker EGT](https://web.arbeitsagentur.de/entgeltatlas/beruf/15637)) | Electrician $29.61/h; plumber $29.59/h; brickmason $28.67/h (BLS 2023) | Skilled day rate about 1.5-2 times the unskilled rate (est.) |
| **Master / supervisor / contractor** | *Meister* course: 1-2 years part-time, about €5-15k, largely covered by Aufstiegs-BAföG (est.). Needed to run a business in many trades | Licensed master electrician, then contractor; construction supervisor $36.90/h (BLS) | Own contracting crew (est.) |
| **Evening school / second-chance education** | Abendschule / Kolleg to get the Abitur: 2-3 years (est.) | GED in months; community college associate degree in 2 years | Private matric/intermediate exams (est.) |
| **Degree** | Bachelor 3 years; public universities have no tuition fees | Bachelor 4 years. With a degree, median $1,578/week vs $966 with only high school (2025) ([BLS Education pays](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/unemployment-earnings-education.htm)) | Bachelor 4 years; public tuition is low, but lost wages are the real cost (est.) |
| **Certifications** | Forklift licence (1-3 days), welding certificates, IHK certificates | CDL truck licence (weeks, pays $26.12/h), CompTIA, OSHA-10 | Driving licence, short IT courses (DigiSkills etc.) (est.) |
| **Small business** | Registering a trade business (*Gewerbe*) is cheap; a *Meister* is required in 41 trades (est.) | LLC setup costs $50-500 (est.) | Kiosk, rickshaw, tailoring or mobile repair; microfinance loans (est.) |

### 2.2 Pay jumps (game tuning anchors)

- **US weekly median earnings by education, 2025** ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/unemployment-earnings-education.htm)). Unemployment falls from 6.1% to 1.8% going down this list.

  | Education level | Median per week |
  |---|---|
  | No high school diploma | $770 |
  | High school | $966 |
  | Some college | $1,062 |
  | Associate degree | $1,135 |
  | Bachelor's degree | $1,578 |
  | Master's degree | $1,876 |
  | Doctorate | $2,307 |

- **US labourer to electrician:** $18.10 to $29.61 an hour, **+64%**, after about 4-5 years.
- **DE labourer to Geselle:** about €14 to about €23 an hour (€3,935/month), **+60%**, after 3-3.5 years.
- **Rough rule for the game:** each real qualification step is worth about +30-60% pay and takes 1-4 years.

### 2.3 Computers and digital skills

- **US:** jobs needing high digital skill paid an average of $72,896, medium $48,274 and low $30,393 (2016).
  - About 60% of tasks in low-digital jobs could be automated, against about 30% in high-digital jobs.
  - Source: [Brookings, "Digitalization and the American workforce"](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/digitalization-and-the-american-workforce/).
- **US ladder examples:** computer user support $28.48/h, software developer $63.59/h (BLS 2023).
- **PK (est.):** a laptop plus internet opens freelancing, call centres, online tutoring and shop digitisation. Pakistan is regularly listed among the top freelancing countries. The limits are power cuts and payment access.
- **Game:** the **computer** should be a physical item. It unlocks a job family (office work, online gigs, running a campaign on social media) and makes the player's jobs harder to automate.

## 4. Helping people and building reputation

- **How many people volunteer**
  - DE: 39.7% of people aged 14+ volunteer (*engagiert*) ([Freiwilligensurvey 2019](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-658-35317-9)).
  - US: 28.3% (75.7 million people) formally volunteered through an organisation in 2023, and many more helped neighbours informally ([Census/AmeriCorps](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2024/11/civic-engagement-and-volunteerism.html)).
- **Where local volunteering happens**
  - DE: *Vereine* (sports clubs, volunteer fire brigade, carnival clubs), church groups, *Bürgervereine* (citizens' associations), parent councils.
  - US: neighbourhood and homeowners' associations, PTAs, churches, block clubs.
  - PK: mohalla (neighbourhood) committees, mosque committees, welfare trusts such as Edhi, traders' associations (est.).
- **How reputation becomes political support** (typical pattern, est.)
  1. Fix visible small problems, or report them repeatedly until they are fixed (sections 3.1-3.2).
  2. Join or lead an association. Associations are the network that nominates local candidates.
  3. Speak at council meetings and run a local initiative, for example a playground, a speed limit or a drainage fix.
  4. Get elected to the council (in DE usually unpaid and honorary) or appointed to a board.
  5. The party, or a group of independent voters (*Freie Wähler*), asks you to stand for mayor.
- **What voters and mayors say matters most** in DE: credibility, closeness to citizens and getting things done. Mayors say their main motives are shaping the town (97%) and enjoying dealing with people (95%) ([Bertelsmann / Forschungsgruppe Wahlen 2008](https://www.forschungsgruppe.de/Umfragen/Archiv__weitere_Umfragen/Buergermeister/)).

**Game:** make reputation per district and per group (for example workers, shop owners, parents, elderly), not one global number. Associations should work as multipliers.

## 5. How people really become mayor

### 5.1 Backgrounds

- **DE**
  - Directly elected full-time mayors are mostly administration professionals.
  - In Baden-Württemberg about **85%** are said to be trained public administrators, many from the administration colleges at Kehl and Ludwigsburg ([Stadtanzeiger Ortenau](https://www.stadtanzeiger-ortenau.de/kehl-stadt/c-extra/mit-buergermeister-schmiede-grosse-aufmerksamkeit-erregt_a32346)). This figure is for one state and comes from a newspaper.
  - A 23-year-old administration student was elected mayor of Oppenweiler in 2025 ([HS Kehl](https://www.hs-kehl.de/hochschule/drei-fragen-an-gabriel-nold/)).
  - Only about 40% of Germany's roughly 10,800 municipalities have a full-time mayor. The rest have honorary (volunteer) mayors ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCrgermeister_(Deutschland))).
- **US**
  - A study of more than 3,000 mayoral candidates in 248 cities over 50 years found that mayors are mostly white-collar and usually already have political experience.
  - **Business owners and executives are about 32% of candidates**, but they do not win more often than others.
  - Source: [Kirkland, "Who Runs for Mayor and Who Wins?"](https://csdp.princeton.edu/publications/representation-american-cities-who-runs-mayor-and-who-wins).
  - Other common routes (est.): lawyer, city council member, state legislator, police or fire chief, non-profit leader.
- **Women** are under-represented: about 9% of mayors in DE ([EAF Berlin 2020](https://www.eaf-berlin.de/was-wir-tun/studien-publikationen/publikation/buergermeisterinnen-und-buergermeister-in-deutschland-30-jahre-nach-der-wiedervereinigung)) and 10.4% in Baden-Württemberg (2024).
- **PK:** mayors of the big cities come from party machines (MQM, PPP, JI in Karachi). Union council chairs are the grassroots level. Local elections are irregular (est.).

### 5.2 Requirements

| | DE (varies by state) | US (varies by city) | PK |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum age | 18 in BW (lowered from 25 in 2023) and in Bavaria; some states 23-25 | Usually 18-25; residency 1-5 years (est.) | 25 for local office (est.) |
| Upper age limit | Being removed in several states ([KOMMUNAL](https://kommunal.de/buergermeister-altersgrenze)) | None | — |
| Citizenship / residency | German or EU citizen. You usually do **not** have to live in the town, since outsiders from the administration often run | Registered voter and resident of the city | Registered voter in the area |
| Nomination | Party list, or independent with supporting signatures, typically a few dozen to a few hundred depending on town size (est.) | Petition signatures, e.g. about 3,750 for a NYC mayoral primary and about 12,500 in Chicago (est., check the current rules); or a filing fee | Party ticket, or independent with nomination papers and a fee (est.) |
| Term | 5-8 years (BW 8, Bavaria 6, NRW 5) | Usually 4 years; term limits are common | 4 years (est.) |

### 5.3 Campaign steps, costs and turnout

- **DE, Baden-Württemberg data** ([Statistisches Landesamt BW, 2025](https://www.statistik-bw.de/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung/durchschnittliche-wahlbeteiligung-bei-buergermeisterwahlen-im-land-bei-knapp-53/)):
  - Average **turnout 52.6%**, ranging from 16.3% to 89.9%.
  - About **2.9 candidates per election**.
  - **27.3% of elections had only one candidate.**
  - Incumbents who ran again won **87.3%** of the time.
  - Winners got an average of **76%** of the vote.
  - Average age of mayors: 49.4.
  - Almost half are in their second or later term.
- **DE campaign cost:**
  - About €10,000-15,000 in a town of 10,000 people ([Freie Wähler BW guide](https://landesverband.freiewaehler.de/bm-ob-werden/buergermeister-in-werden/kosten-eines-bm-wahlkampfes/)).
  - A common rule of thumb is €1-2 per resident ([buergermeisterin.de](https://buergermeisterin.de/der-wahlkampf-kann-beginnen/)).
  - The candidate often pays much of it personally.
- **US turnout:** about **20%** on average in the 30 largest cities, as low as 6.1% in Dallas and 14% in NYC in that cycle ([Harvard Data-Smart on Portland State's "Who Votes for Mayor?"](https://datasmart.hks.harvard.edu/news/article/map-of-the-month-who-votes-for-mayor-1157)). Turnout is higher when the city votes on the same day as a presidential election.
- **US campaign cost (est.):**
  - Small town: $1,000-20,000.
  - Mid-size city: $100k to $1M.
  - Big city: tens of millions (NYC offers public matching funds).
- **Why DE and US differ.** A DE mayor is a directly elected chief executive who runs the town administration, and elections are often cross-party and personality-driven. In a US strong-mayor city the mayor is a partisan politician. In council-manager cities the mayor chairs the council and a hired city manager runs the administration.

## 6. What a mayor actually does

### 6.1 Budget breakdown

**DE, all municipalities combined, 2025** ([Destatis PM 114/2026](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/04/PD26_114_71137.html)). Total spending €423.3 bn.

| Spending type | € bn | % of total |
|---|---|---|
| Staff | 113.4 | 26.8% |
| Running costs (buildings, energy, services) | 99.1 | 23.4% |
| Social benefits (disability integration €25.2 bn, youth welfare €20.0 bn) | 90.0 | 21.3% |
| Investment (of which construction €38.0 bn) | 53.9 | 12.7% |
| Other (transfers, interest, etc.) | ≈66.9 | ≈15.8% |

- **Income:** €391.4 bn, of which taxes €136.5 bn and property tax (*Grundsteuer*) €14.5 bn.
- **Deficit:** a **record €31.9 bn**. 7.5% of spending was not covered and was financed with short-term cash loans.

**US city general fund (est.):**
- Public safety (police, fire, EMS) usually takes **50-65%**.
- Austin's FY2026 general fund is about 64% public safety, according to a [dashboard built from city data](https://austin311.com/budget/).
- Across all US local governments, which include school districts, schools dominate at about 39% of direct spending, police about 6% and roads about 4% ([Urban Institute](https://www.urban.org/policy-centers/cross-center-initiatives/state-and-local-finance-initiative/state-and-local-backgrounders/state-and-local-expenditures)).

### 6.2 The main levers

1. **Budget proposal.** The mayor or administration drafts it; the council adopts it.
2. **Zoning and building permits.** Land-use plans (*Bebauungsplan*) in DE are adopted by council vote.
3. **Services.** Waste collection frequency, street maintenance, parks, libraries, childcare places.
4. **Staff.** Hiring, organisation, who heads each department. In a DE town the mayor is head of the whole administration.
5. **Fees and local taxes.** Property-tax and business-tax multipliers in DE, fees, parking charges. These need a council vote.
6. **Grants and funding.** Chasing state, federal and EU funding is a big part of a DE mayor's work (est.).
7. **Convening and public attention.** Round tables, the mayor's office hours for citizens, the press.

### 6.3 The constraints

- **Council votes.** The mayor needs a majority for the budget, fees and plans.
- **Higher law.** 72% of DE mayors name over-regulation by the state, federal government and EU as their biggest obstacle, and 49% name the town's finances ([Bertelsmann 2008](https://www.forschungsgruppe.de/Umfragen/Archiv__weitere_Umfragen/Buergermeister/)).
- **Debt limits.**
  - DE state supervisory authorities (*Kommunalaufsicht*) must approve loans. Towns in deficit must follow a budget-recovery plan (*Haushaltssicherungskonzept*).
  - US cities are bound by balanced-budget rules and credit ratings. Detroit went bankrupt in 2013 (est.).
- **The next election.** Terms are 4-8 years, and incumbents who run win about 87% of the time in BW, so mistakes cost less than players might expect.
- **Personal cost.** 80% of DE mayors say family and private life suffer, and 41% say the load has hurt their health (Bertelsmann 2008).

### 6.4 Typical crises (examples, est. figures)

| Crisis | Real example | Game effect |
|---|---|---|
| Flood / heavy rain | Ahr valley, DE, 2021 (about 135 dead); Pakistan floods 2022 (1,700+ dead, about 33M affected) | Emergency spending, rebuilding for years, lasting reputation effect |
| Heat wave | Karachi 2015 (about 1,200+ dead); thousands of heat deaths per summer in DE | Cooling centres, water points, tree planting |
| Budget shortfall | DE municipalities' record deficit in 2025 (above); Detroit bankruptcy 2013 | Cut services or raise taxes, both lose support |
| Strikes | ver.di public-sector strikes (DE 2023, 2025); sanitation strikes | Uncollected rubbish, closed daycare; pay rises strain the budget |
| Scandal | Corruption and awarding contracts to allies (common everywhere) | Trust crash, investigation, possible recall |
| Water or utility failure | Flint, US, 2014; Karachi water shortage | Health, protests, very costly repairs |

## 7. Higher office, up to president

### 7.1 The realistic ladder

council → mayor → state or regional office → national parliament → minister or governor → head of government or state

Examples of leaders who started locally:
- **Konrad Adenauer:** Lord Mayor of Cologne (1917-33), later the first Federal Chancellor (1949).
- **Willy Brandt:** Governing Mayor of Berlin (1957-66), then Foreign Minister, then Chancellor (1969).
- **Olaf Scholz:** First Mayor of Hamburg (2011-18), then Finance Minister, then Chancellor (2021).
- **Grover Cleveland:** Mayor of Buffalo (1882), Governor of New York (1883), US President (1885). The fastest real climb, about 3 years.
- **Calvin Coolidge:** city council, then Mayor of Northampton, then state senate, then Lieutenant Governor, then Governor of Massachusetts, then Vice-President, then President. Almost the full ladder.
- **Recep Tayyip Erdoğan:** Mayor of Istanbul (1994), then Prime Minister (2003), then President of Turkey (2014).
- **Joko Widodo:** Mayor of Surakarta (2005), then Governor of Jakarta (2012), then President of Indonesia (2014).
- **Jacques Chirac:** Mayor of Paris (1977-95), then President of France (1995).
- **Mauricio Macri:** Head of Government of Buenos Aires (2007), then President of Argentina (2015).
- **PK and IN** tend to climb through provincial office instead:
  - Nawaz Sharif: Punjab finance minister, then Chief Minister, then Prime Minister.
  - Narendra Modi: Chief Minister of Gujarat, then Prime Minister.

**Typical timeline (est.):** 10-25 years from first council seat to national office. Most people stop at mayor or state level.

### 7.2 How national elections work (broad strokes)

- **US:** party primaries, then a general election. The president is chosen by the Electoral College: 538 electors, 270 needed to win. Congress is elected separately.
- **DE:** the Bundestag is elected with two votes, one for a local candidate and one for a party list. Parties need at least 5% to get seats.
  - The **Chancellor** is elected by a majority of the Bundestag, usually through a coalition.
  - The **Federal President** is largely ceremonial and is elected by the Federal Convention.
- **PK:** the National Assembly is elected by first-past-the-post in single-seat districts, plus reserved seats.
  - The **Prime Minister** is elected by the National Assembly.
  - The **President** is elected by an electoral college of parliament and the provincial assemblies.
- **IN:** the Lok Sabha has 543 seats. The Prime Minister leads the majority, and the President is largely ceremonial.

**Game:** "President" can mean the *head of government*: US president, DE chancellor, PK or IN prime minister. Offer a country-flavour switch that changes the final title and whether coalitions matter.
